# Support Speed with Substance

We are preparing to accept support through GitHub Sponsors. Sponsorship is not yet live.

## Profile copy

I'm Yuan Lin, the maintainer of Speed with Substance: an open project for measuring both the quality and speed of LLM inference recipes.

Fast inference is useful only when the results remain trustworthy. We are developing a versioned benchmark and reproducible testing process that compares supplied recipes against an original-precision reference, measures quality and serving behavior, and reports speed alongside quality-qualified goodput.

The initial scope is a single NVIDIA DGX Spark. We publish only results we test and verify ourselves, with runnable benchmark packages and raw evidence so others can reproduce our findings.

Sponsorship will support maintainer time, benchmark development and maintenance, test execution and investigation, reference generation, storage, and operating costs. With sufficient support, we would like to extend the work to multi-node configurations under a separately defined test profile.

The project currently contains a methodology proposal, a reference utility, a benchmark-command wrapper, and an empty results board. There are no measured benchmark results yet. The first funding milestone will be a costed plan for the initial benchmark release and testing campaign; no delivery date or test volume is promised until that plan is published.

## Independence and public access

- Funding supports the work, never a particular outcome or ranking.
- Benchmark code, methodology, and published results will remain publicly accessible. Data that cannot be redistributed will require documented acquisition instructions.
- Published evaluations will include failed and inconclusive outcomes, not just passing recipes.
- Financial sponsors and donated or loaned hardware will be disclosed, subject to sponsors' privacy preferences. Relevant conflicts of interest will be disclosed with results.
- Supporters may suggest testing priorities. The published benchmark and evaluation rules govern the results; sponsorship provides no editorial veto.
